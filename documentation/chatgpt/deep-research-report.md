# Proposal for the Canadian Metaresearch Data Challenge

## What the challenge demands

The call is narrower than it first appears. It is not asking for a generic bibliometric map of Canadian scholarship. It is asking for a methodology to construct an **open, reproducible, and inclusive dataset** of the **Canadian metaresearch landscape**, with explicit attention to diverse traditions such as metascience, bibliometrics, scientometrics, STS, research integrity, reproducibility research, scholarly communication, and open science research. The form also makes the constraint plain: the substantive response is a **maximum two-page attachment** describing methodology, openness and reproducibility, diversity and bias considerations, and a brief work plan. Selected teams must implement the method before the conference, produce an openly available dataset and documentation, share code and workflows where feasible, and present in a 30-minute plenary session. The deadline is **July 15, 2026**, notifications are due **August 1, 2026**, and up to **CAD $4,000** is available for participation costs. citeturn12view0

That means a weak proposal would be one that promises a flashy map without a defensible boundary around what counts as metaresearch, or one that assumes a single OpenAlex query can capture the field. The selection criteria reward methodological rigour, feasibility, reproducibility, inclusiveness, originality, and clarity of outputs. So the winning move is not “more analytics.” It is a **carefully validated retrieval and documentation pipeline** that treats the field as plural, bilingual, and partially invisible to any one database. citeturn12view0turn15view3turn16view0

## What a strong proposal needs

The strongest strategy is to frame Canadian metaresearch as a **plural field of research-on-research**, not as a single discipline. Ioannidis defines meta-research as the study of research itself, including methods, reporting, reproducibility, evaluation, and incentives, and explicitly describes it as interdisciplinary. That aligns cleanly with the challenge’s invitation to include bibliometrics, STS, scholarly communication, open scholarship, and research integrity traditions in one design rather than forcing them into a narrow metascience-only definition. citeturn15view3turn12view0

OpenAlex is the right core source because the challenge itself points applicants toward it, and because OpenAlex provides open metadata on works, authors, institutions, funders, and topics, with both an API and downloadable snapshots. Its documentation supports exactly the operations this challenge needs: filtering, grouping, cursor paging, and bulk download. OpenAlex also positions itself as a more inclusive and open alternative to proprietary indexes, with stronger coverage of non-English works and works from the Global South. citeturn12view0turn2view0turn3view0turn3view1turn4view0

But this is where most proposals become sloppy. OpenAlex is useful, not magical. Evidence suggests it can be a reliable alternative for some bibliometric analyses, especially at the country level, yet the same literature also warns about metadata accuracy and completeness limits. A recent comparison concluded that OpenAlex is a superset of Scopus and can be reliable for some analyses, particularly at country level, while still requiring additional work on metadata quality. Separate evaluation found that OpenAlex includes affiliation data for 61% of publications and institution data for 49%, and linguistic coverage studies found more balanced language coverage than Web of Science but also warned that language metadata are not always accurate. That combination is exactly why your proposal must promise **validation and bias auditing**, not mere retrieval. citeturn15view0turn9search2turn8search2

There is also a Canadian reason to lean into this. A recent Canadian knowledge synthesis argued that OpenAlex provides new insights into Canadian research outputs, includes more non-English publications relative to Web of Science and Scopus, and can therefore offer a more accurate positioning of Canada in the global research system. That is useful for a challenge explicitly concerned with Canadian coverage and multilingual inclusiveness. citeturn8search1

Finally, the proposal should tie openness to **FAIR** and inclusiveness to **CARE**, and in the Canadian context explicitly acknowledge **OCAP®** principles where First Nations data governance issues arise. FAIR calls for data and the workflows that generate them to be findable, accessible, interoperable, and reusable. CARE complements FAIR by centring collective benefit, authority to control, responsibility, and ethics in Indigenous data governance, while OCAP® establishes First Nations principles of ownership, control, access, and possession. If you ignore that governance layer and treat openness as “dump everything online,” the proposal becomes technically open but methodologically immature. citeturn7search2turn7search3turn13search1

## Submission-ready proposal draft

### Project title

**Mapping Canadian Metaresearch as a Plural Field: A Bilingual, OpenAlex-Based, Reproducible, and Bias-Audited Dataset**

### Draft text

We propose to develop an open, reproducible, and inclusive methodology for identifying and mapping the Canadian metaresearch landscape as a **plural field of research-on-research**. We define metaresearch broadly, drawing on established work that describes it as the study of research itself, including methods, reporting, reproducibility, evaluation, and incentives, and we align that definition with the challenge’s explicit invitation to include bibliometrics, scientometrics, STS, research integrity, scholarly communication, and open scholarship traditions. Rather than assuming that one discipline, one language, or one keyword set defines the field, our approach treats Canadian metaresearch as a heterogeneous ecosystem that must be mapped across multiple research cultures and methodological traditions. citeturn15view3turn12view0

Our core data source will be OpenAlex because it is open, programmatically accessible, and supports analysis of works, authors, institutions, funders, and topics through filters, grouping, and bulk data access. We will use OpenAlex as the backbone of the dataset, but not as an unquestioned ground truth. Prior research indicates that OpenAlex is suitable for some bibliometric uses, particularly at the country level, while still presenting limitations in metadata completeness and accuracy. Our methodology is therefore designed around **iterative retrieval plus explicit validation**, rather than around a single-pass search. citeturn2view0turn3view0turn3view1turn4view0turn15view0turn9search2

The dataset will be constructed in four stages. First, we will create a **bilingual seed set and vocabulary** using English and French terms associated with metaresearch and adjacent traditions, including metaresearch, metascience, bibliometrics, scientometrics, scholarly communication, open science, research integrity, reproducibility, peer review, research evaluation, and their French equivalents. We will supplement this lexical approach with a small, manually curated seed corpus of known metaresearch-relevant works and authors in Canada. Second, we will retrieve a broad candidate corpus from OpenAlex using combinations of search, filter, and aboutness tools, including Canadian affiliation filters and text-based tagging. Third, we will score and classify candidate records using transparent inclusion and exclusion rules. Fourth, we will validate the resulting dataset through manual review, coverage checks, and bias audits. citeturn12view0turn3view0turn5view0turn6view1

Our inclusion logic will be deliberately plural. A record will be considered in scope when it studies research practices, research systems, scholarly communication, research assessment, reproducibility, openness, or related infrastructures; and when it is linked to Canada either through at least one Canadian-affiliated author, a substantial empirical focus on the Canadian research system, or a clear connection to Canadian institutions, infrastructures, or funding contexts. This design avoids the most common failure in landscape mapping: equating “Canadian metaresearch” only with work authored in Canada, while missing work about Canada produced elsewhere, or equating metaresearch only with mainstream metascience while missing STS and scholarly communication contributions. That broader scope is justified by the challenge call itself and by longstanding evidence that narrower bibliometric databases and approaches can systematically underrepresent social sciences, arts and humanities, and non-English scholarship. citeturn12view0turn16view0turn15view0

To make the dataset reproducible, we will publish the full retrieval and processing workflow, including query logs, inclusion rules, code, environment specification, versioned releases, and a data dictionary. We will structure the released materials so that others can reproduce the dataset from OpenAlex metadata and inspect every decision point, from candidate generation to final inclusion. This design follows FAIR principles for data and workflows and aligns with Canadian research data management and open access expectations, which emphasize sound stewardship and broad dissemination of research outputs. citeturn7search2turn11search0turn11search16

To make the dataset inclusive, we will explicitly audit for underrepresentation across languages, disciplines, and research traditions. OpenAlex offers broader linguistic coverage than Web of Science, and recent Canadian work suggests it improves visibility of non-English Canadian outputs, but language metadata remain imperfect. We will therefore stratify validation by language and by research tradition, with special attention to French-language scholarship and to traditions that are easily missed by keyword-dependent retrieval, especially STS, humanities-adjacent work, and infrastructure or practitioner-oriented contributions. Where records concern Indigenous data governance or related communities, we will apply CARE principles and respect Canadian OCAP®-style governance expectations, avoiding careless release of derived labels or community-sensitive inferences. citeturn8search2turn8search1turn7search3turn13search1

Validation will be central to the project. We will estimate precision through manual annotation of a stratified random sample of included records, estimate recall by checking whether curated seed records and nominated exemplars are recovered, and analyse false positives and false negatives by language, discipline, publication type, and institutional context. We will also document the limitations of OpenAlex metadata, especially missing or incomplete affiliation data and imperfect language assignment, rather than hiding those weaknesses behind aggregate visualisations. citeturn9search2turn8search2turn15view0

Our analytical outputs will include a documented dataset of Canadian metaresearch records; descriptive summaries by year, language, institution, and topic; co-authorship and institutional collaboration maps; citation and bibliometric indicators presented with appropriate cautions; and an exploratory map of infrastructures and funding relationships using available OpenAlex funder and award metadata. These outputs match the call’s suggested components while remaining feasible within the challenge timeline because the work is built on open infrastructure and a staged validation design rather than on bespoke closed data collection. citeturn12view0turn5view0

Our work plan is designed to finish before the conference. In the first phase, we will build the vocabulary, seed corpus, and retrieval scripts. In the second phase, we will generate the candidate corpus, conduct iterative refinement, and complete the first validation round. In the third phase, we will finalise the release package, produce analytical outputs, and prepare the plenary presentation. The final public package will include the dataset, documentation, code, validation protocol, and a concise methodological report describing assumptions, sources of bias, and recommended uses and non-uses of the data. citeturn12view0turn4view0turn3view1

## Implementation details that make the proposal credible

If you want the committee to take this seriously, show that you understand OpenAlex at a technical level. OpenAlex supports filters in `attribute:value` form, with AND via commas, OR via pipes, and repeated filters for intersection on the same attribute. For works, it supports fields such as `publication_year`, `language`, `type`, `institutions.country_code`, `authorships.institutions.country_code`, `awards.funder_id`, and `open_access.is_oa`, while grouped counts can be produced with `group_by`. The documentation also makes clear that standard paging only reaches the first 10,000 records and that cursor paging or bulk downloads are required for larger result sets. For large-scale local analysis, the OpenAlex snapshot or official CLI is the more sensible route. citeturn3view0turn5view0turn6view0turn3view1turn4view0

In other words, do not write a vague sentence about “using OpenAlex.” Write that you will use **bilingual search plus structured filters**, then move to **bulk local processing** when candidate volume becomes large. Also note that OpenAlex’s `/text/keywords` endpoint can be used to tag free text with OpenAlex keywords, which is useful for harmonising vocabulary across English and French seed records, though it is rate-limited and should be used selectively rather than as the main retrieval engine. That level of detail signals feasibility without pretending the workflow will be trivial. citeturn6view1turn4view0

A sensible operational split is this. Use the API during scoping and refinement. Use bulk download or the CLI for the final extraction and reproducible freeze. Release the final dataset in tidy tabular form plus a richer machine-readable form, with a schema, extraction date, OpenAlex source version, transformation log, and explicit confidence labels on records. That is the difference between a project plan and a real data methodology. citeturn4view0turn3view1turn7search2

## Limitations and bias statement you should not omit

This proposal will be stronger if it admits what it cannot fully solve. First, any OpenAlex-based approach inherits the strengths and weaknesses of the underlying metadata. Affiliation data are incomplete for a substantial share of records, and language metadata, while more balanced than Web of Science, are not perfectly accurate. So Canadian linkage and bilingual coverage must be treated as probabilistic, not perfect. citeturn9search2turn8search2turn15view0

Second, bibliometric databases and search strategies can distort the field they claim to represent. Classic evidence on Web of Science and Scopus showed systematic overrepresentation of natural sciences, biomedicine, and English-language journals, with corresponding underrepresentation of social sciences, arts and humanities, and other languages. OpenAlex improves openness and inclusiveness, but that does not eliminate the need for audits of what remains undercaptured, particularly STS, humanities-facing scholarship, practitioner literature, and non-English work. citeturn16view0turn15view0turn8search1

Third, “inclusive” does not simply mean “more records.” It also means avoiding careless treatment of communities whose data have governance expectations beyond generic openness. FAIR alone is insufficient where Indigenous data rights and collective interests are implicated; CARE and, in the First Nations context, OCAP® principles provide a better governance frame. Your proposal should therefore state that openness will be pursued **as openly as possible, but not more openly than is ethical**. citeturn7search2turn7search3turn13search1turn10search21

## Why this version is competitive

This draft is competitive because it answers the exact judging rubric rather than dancing around it. It is methodologically rigorous because it defines scope, retrieval, inclusion logic, and validation. It is feasible because it uses OpenAlex’s existing infrastructure and avoids promising exhaustive manual curation of the entire field. It is reproducible because it commits to sharing queries, code, workflows, documentation, and versioned outputs. It is inclusive because it treats metaresearch as a plural field, incorporates English and French, and explicitly audits bias instead of pretending neutrality. And it is original because it proposes a **bias-audited, confidence-scored, bilingual mapping pipeline**, not just a descriptive dashboard. Those are exactly the dimensions the challenge says it will evaluate. citeturn12view0turn15view0turn7search2turn7search3

The blunt version is this: do not submit a proposal that promises “topic modelling and network analysis” as if those were the method. They are outputs. The method is the **construction of a defensible dataset**. This version understands that distinction, and that is why it is much closer to what the committee is actually asking for. citeturn12view0turn15view3turn16view0
