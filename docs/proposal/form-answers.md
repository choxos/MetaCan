**Relevant work expertise**

> Metaresearch, evidence synthesis and research-transparency methods, with a decade of published work on how
> research is reported, shared and retracted. Relevant to this challenge in four specific ways.
>
> **I have measured what automated bibliographic retrieval misses.** In a study of publicly funded trials I
> benchmarked automated publication linkage against a manual reference standard built by two independent
> reviewers: the matcher was accurate on the records that reached it (sensitivity 92.2%, PPV 94.3%), but overall
> sensitivity was 44.5%. The loss was in retrieval, not matching. That result is why this proposal treats
> retrieval error as a measured outcome rather than a limitation paragraph.
>
> **I build validated automated classifiers for exactly this kind of text.** I am the author of `rtransparency`,
> an R package detecting ten transparency indicators (data and code availability, conflicts, funding, protocol
> registration, reporting guidelines, AI-use disclosure) in full text, with published sensitivity and specificity
> against a human-labelled benchmark, and detectors operating across six languages. I also wrote `rfair`, a native
> R implementation of FAIR assessment. Both are open source with Zenodo DOIs.
>
> **I have run screening and adjudication pipelines at national scale.** For a study of Canadian health research
> funding I ingested 44,190 CIHR awards and ran a two-independent-reviewer adjudication pipeline with formal
> agreement statistics and a gold-standard validation sample, alongside institution-to-province resolution across
> all thirteen provinces and territories. I have done the Canadian-metadata plumbing this challenge requires.
>
> **I build and operate open scholarly databases, and I have built one for a community that indexes miss.** I run
> eight public open-science databases (including `prct.xeradb.com`, a post-retraction citation tracker, and
> `ost.xeradb.com`, a transparency tracker over ~2.7M papers). One of them, `kurdishscholars.xeradb.com`, is an
> OpenAlex-backed, community-confirmed directory of scholars from a stateless, multilingual community, built
> because no database would otherwise represent them. Mapping a community that standard bibliometrics renders
> partially invisible is the problem I have already worked on, and the reason I want this one.
>
> Selected relevant publications: research transparency across 59 fields of medicine and health; FAIR assessment
> of dental and COVID-19 research data (*J Dent Res* 2022; *PLOS ONE* 2024); transparency of COVID-19 research

**Code and data (live now, before selection):**
`https://github.com/choxos/CaRN-data-challenge`

Every figure quoted in the attachment is regenerated by `make pilot`, and re-derivable **with no network at all** by
`make pilot-offline`, from raw API responses and screening labels archived in the repository. The committee does not
have to take the pilot on trust.

And the attachment cannot drift from the pilot, because the build refuses to let it: `make proposal` runs
`pilot/check_proposal_numbers.R`, which fails if the attachment quotes any figure the pilot does not produce. That
guard exists because an earlier draft claimed the topic route "covers a third of the field" while the pilot said 12%,
and nothing caught it.

**Every error this project has made is recorded in `DEVIATIONS.md`, written before submission rather than after.**
There are twenty-six entries. They include a retracted coverage figure; a claim of *differential* bias against STS and LIS
that rested on four records and a non-significant interaction; a hard-coded sentence about francophone coverage that
turned out to be backwards; a cost model that overstated the screening bill fivefold and thereby talked me into a
design I did not need; an audit design that could not measure what an audit exists to measure; and, worst,
**a pilot screen that did not obey its own locked rubric** (it was sent six of the eight fields the rubric mandates).

I would rather the committee read that file than not. A project whose thesis is *measure what you missed* has to
survive its own instrument, and the only honest way to show that it did is to show where it did not.
